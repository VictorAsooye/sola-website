How Much Does a Custom Business App Cost? A Real Answer

JUL 31, 2026 · Sola AI Consulting

The Question Nobody Gives a Straight Answer To

If you've searched "how much does a custom business app cost" and found nothing but vague ranges like "$5,000 to $500,000," you're not alone. That answer is technically true and completely useless at the same time.

The real answer depends on what kind of app you actually need. Not an app in the abstract. Your app — the one that solves a specific operational problem inside your specific business.

At Sola AI Consulting, we've built internal portals, scheduling tools, intake systems, and AI-powered automations for small businesses and home health agencies. So let's talk real numbers, and more importantly, what drives them.

What You're Actually Paying For

Most business owners think they're buying code. What they're actually buying is someone else's problem-solving time. That distinction matters because it changes how you evaluate every quote you receive.

Custom app development costs break into a few real categories:

  • Discovery and scoping — figuring out what you actually need, not what you asked for
  • Design — how users move through the app, not just how it looks
  • Development — building the logic, connecting systems, writing the code
  • Integration — connecting to your existing tools like QuickBooks, EMR systems, or scheduling platforms
  • Testing and launch — making sure it doesn't fall apart in real use
  • Ongoing support — updates, fixes, and changes as your business evolves

Every quote you get should account for all of these. If someone gives you a price that only covers development, you're going to be surprised later.

Platform vs. Custom Build

One of the first decisions that shapes cost is whether you're building from scratch or building on top of an existing platform. A SharePoint intranet with custom logic is not the same project as a full custom-coded web application. Neither is better by default — it depends entirely on your situation.

For most small businesses, platform-based solutions like SharePoint, Power Apps, or Notion-connected automations will cost significantly less and get you to production faster. Full custom builds make sense when your workflow is complex enough that off-the-shelf tools genuinely can't handle it.

"The most expensive thing we see isn't overbuilding — it's rebuilding. A business spends $8,000 on a tool that doesn't fit their process, then spends another $12,000 fixing it six months later. Getting the scope right upfront is the cheapest thing you can do."

Real Cost Ranges, Broken Down by Project Type

Here's where we get specific. These are ballpark ranges based on actual project work, not industry surveys written by people who've never shipped anything.

Internal Portals and Intranets ($3,000 – $15,000)

If you need a central place for your team to access documents, submit forms, track tasks, or communicate — a SharePoint-based internal portal can do all of that. For a home health agency, this might mean a caregiver portal with shift schedules, HR documents, and a place to log visit notes. For a small business, it could be a simple staff hub with policies, announcements, and onboarding checklists.

On the lower end, you're getting a clean, functional build with standard features. On the higher end, you're adding custom workflows, permission structures, and integrations with other tools your team already uses.

Workflow Automations and AI Tools ($1,500 – $8,000)

This is the category that's grown the most in the last two years. Automations that route intake forms, generate documents, send follow-up messages, or summarize data from spreadsheets — these don't require a massive custom app. They require smart configuration of tools like Power Automate, Zapier, or OpenAI's API stitched together with your existing systems.

A well-built automation can eliminate hours of manual work per week. We've seen home health agencies cut their intake processing time by more than half with a properly scoped AI workflow — built in under $5,000.

Full Custom Web or Mobile Applications ($15,000 – $80,000+)

If you need something genuinely bespoke — a client-facing portal, a custom scheduling engine, a mobile app your field staff uses daily — you're in this range. The wide spread is real. A simple CRUD app (create, read, update, delete records) sits on the lower end. Add real-time data, complex permission logic, mobile optimization, third-party API integrations, and robust testing, and costs climb fast.

This is also where many small businesses overbuild. If you're not sure you need this level of investment, you probably don't — yet.

Key takeaway

Before asking "how much will this cost," ask "what problem am I solving and how often does it happen?" If you can't answer that clearly, no developer can give you an accurate quote — and any number they give you will be wrong.

What Makes Costs Go Up (And How to Control Them)

Three things reliably push custom app costs higher than expected.

Scope creep

It starts with a simple idea. Then someone asks, "Can it also do X?" three weeks into the build. Then Y. Then Z. Every addition is reasonable on its own. Together, they can double your project cost. The fix is a detailed scope document before a single line of code is written — and a clear process for handling change requests.

Unclear requirements

Developers charge for their time, including the time spent figuring out what you meant. Vague requirements lead to rework. Rework leads to invoices you didn't expect. The more clearly you can describe how your current process works and where it breaks down, the more accurate your estimate will be.

Choosing the wrong partner

A freelancer who underbids to win the work, then disappears when problems surface, ends up costing more than a higher-priced firm with a track record. Ask to see live work they've built. Ask who maintains it after launch. Ask what happens if something breaks three months in.

Custom app pricing is one of those areas where the cheapest option and the best value are rarely the same thing.

So, What Should You Budget?

For most small businesses asking this question for the first time, a realistic starting budget for a useful, functional internal tool or automation is somewhere between $3,000 and $10,000. That gets you something real — not a prototype, not a demo, but a working system your team can use immediately.

If you're a home health agency trying to modernize intake, scheduling, or staff communication, that range gets you surprisingly far when the solution is scoped correctly from the start.

The goal isn't to spend the least amount possible. The goal is to spend the right amount on the right solution — and to stop paying for inefficiency with your team's time instead.
