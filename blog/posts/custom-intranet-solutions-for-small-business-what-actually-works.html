Custom Intranet Solutions for Small Business: What Actually Works

SEP 04, 2026 · Sola AI Consulting

Why Off-the-Shelf Intranet Tools Keep Letting Small Businesses Down

Most small businesses don't have an intranet problem. They have a scattered information problem. Policies live in someone's email. The onboarding checklist is a Word doc on a desktop that only one person can find. Shift schedules go out in a group text. New hires spend their first week asking questions that should already have answers.

The typical fix is to grab a tool — Microsoft Teams, Google Sites, maybe a basic SharePoint template — and hope it sticks. It rarely does. Not because those tools are bad, but because they weren't built around how your business actually runs. A home health agency operates nothing like a landscaping company or a freight broker. A generic template can't know that.

That's the core argument for a custom intranet solution for small business: it's built around your workflows, your terminology, your staff, and your compliance requirements. Not around someone else's assumptions about what you need.

"The biggest waste I see isn't money — it's time. When a caregiver can't find the updated medication policy before a visit, that's a crisis waiting to happen. A well-built intranet prevents that. A generic one just adds another place to look."

What a Custom Small Business Intranet Actually Includes

The phrase "custom intranet" can sound expensive and complex. In practice, for a small business with 10 to 150 employees, it usually comes down to a focused set of features built on a platform you already pay for — most often Microsoft SharePoint or a combination of SharePoint and Power Apps.

A Single Front Door for Your Business

The most immediate win is consolidation. A well-designed internal portal gives every employee one place to start their day. From there they can access their schedule, find the latest HR documents, submit a form, or check announcements from leadership. No more hunting across three apps and a shared drive.

At Sola AI Consulting, we've built these portals for home health agencies where the front page shows different content depending on whether you're a field caregiver, an office coordinator, or a billing specialist. Role-based content targeting isn't a luxury — for regulated industries, it's essential.

Document Management That People Actually Use

A custom intranet solution for small business should make the right document findable in under 30 seconds. That means structured libraries, not just a pile of folders. It means version control so staff aren't accidentally referencing a policy from two years ago. And it means search that works — with filters by department, document type, or date.

We've seen agencies where compliance audits were painful because documents were scattered across email threads and personal drives. After moving to a structured SharePoint intranet, the same audit took a fraction of the time. The documents were already organized. The timestamps were already there.

Forms, Workflows, and Light Automation

This is where a custom build earns its cost. Most small businesses are running manual processes that could be partially automated — incident reports emailed around instead of submitted through a form that routes to the right person, time-off requests handled in a spreadsheet, new hire checklists that exist only in someone's memory.

A custom intranet can include Power Automate flows that trigger on form submission, send notifications, log entries to a SharePoint list, and flag items for review. These aren't enterprise-grade automations. They're small, practical time-savers that compound over months.

Key takeaway

Before you design a single page of your intranet, spend 30 minutes listing every process that currently lives in someone's head, email inbox, or personal phone. That list is your build roadmap. The best intranet feature is the one that replaces a daily friction point your staff already complains about.

How to Think About Cost and Scope as a Small Business

One reason small businesses avoid custom intranet solutions is the assumption that "custom" means expensive. It can — but it doesn't have to. The key is scoping honestly and building in phases.

Start With What's Broken Right Now

You don't need to build everything at once. A Phase 1 intranet might just be a clean homepage, a document library, and one automated workflow for a high-volume process like onboarding or incident reporting. That alone can deliver measurable time savings within the first month.

Phase 2 might add a staff directory, a training portal, or a department-specific hub for your billing team. Each phase builds on the last, and you're never paying for features you're not ready to use.

Leverage What You Already Have

If your business already pays for Microsoft 365, you have SharePoint, Power Apps, and Power Automate included in most plans. A custom intranet solution for small business built on these tools doesn't require new software licenses. You're paying for design, configuration, and the strategic thinking about how to structure your information — not for another SaaS subscription.

This matters more than most small business owners realize. The total cost of ownership for a SharePoint-based intranet is significantly lower than a standalone intranet platform, especially when you factor in the integrations that come free with the Microsoft ecosystem.

What Makes an Intranet Stick Long-Term

Here's the honest part that most vendors skip: the technology is the easy half. The harder half is adoption.

A custom intranet solution only delivers value when your staff actually uses it. That means the design has to be intuitive enough that a part-time caregiver with limited tech experience can navigate it on their phone without a training manual. It means leadership has to model the behavior — posting announcements there, not in a group chat. And it means the content has to stay current, or people will stop trusting it.

We build every client portal with an admin layer that a non-technical owner or office manager can maintain. No developers needed to update a policy document or add a new employee to the directory. If updating the intranet requires a help ticket, it won't get updated — and a stale intranet is worse than no intranet because it erodes trust.

The businesses that get the most from a custom intranet are the ones who treat it like infrastructure, not a project. It's not something you build and walk away from. It's something you tend, like a good process manual — kept current, kept honest, kept useful.

If you're running a small business and you're tired of information chaos, misaligned teams, and compliance headaches caused by bad document management, a custom-built intranet is worth a serious look. The right build partner will ask more questions than they answer at first — because the point is to solve your actual problems, not to sell you a template with your logo on it.
