What It Really Costs to Outsource IT for Small Business

AUG 31, 2026 · Sola AI Consulting

The Real Cost of Outsourcing IT — and Why Most Estimates Miss the Point

When small business owners search for "outsource IT for small business cost," they usually find a wide range of numbers and walk away more confused than when they started. You'll see quotes like "$50/month per user" and "$150/hour for break-fix support" thrown around without any context. Those numbers aren't wrong — they're just incomplete.

The real cost of outsourcing IT depends on three things: what you actually need, how your business runs day-to-day, and whether you're buying reactive support or proactive infrastructure. Most small businesses are paying for the first two while desperately needing the third.

"The cheapest IT support is the kind that prevents the problem before your team ever notices it. Most small businesses don't find out they needed that until after something breaks."

Let's break this down practically, the way we do it with clients at Sola AI Consulting before we ever talk about a proposal.

Common Pricing Models and What You're Actually Getting

There are three standard ways managed IT providers price their services. Understanding the difference changes how you evaluate any quote you receive.

Break-Fix (Hourly)

This is the most common entry point for small businesses. Something breaks, you call someone, you pay by the hour. Rates typically run between $75 and $200 per hour depending on your market and the complexity of the work. For a 5-person office with basic needs, this might cost you $500 to $1,500 per year — or it might cost you $8,000 in one bad month when your server goes down before payroll.

Break-fix is unpredictable. It also creates a quiet incentive problem: the provider only gets paid when things go wrong. That's not the relationship you want for something as critical as your business technology.

Managed Services (Per User or Per Device)

A managed service provider (MSP) charges a flat monthly fee — usually $75 to $175 per user per month for small businesses — and in return, they handle monitoring, updates, security patches, helpdesk support, and often backup management. For a 10-person team, that's roughly $750 to $1,750 per month, or $9,000 to $21,000 annually.

That sounds like a lot until you factor in what you're replacing: a part-time IT person, software licensing, downtime costs, and the mental overhead of managing tech yourself.

Project-Based or Hybrid

Some businesses — especially home health agencies and small professional services firms — don't need round-the-clock support. They need someone to build the right infrastructure once, then step in periodically when something changes. This hybrid model can run anywhere from $2,500 to $15,000 for an initial build-out, with smaller monthly retainers afterward. It's often the most cost-effective model if you have stable operations and a low volume of day-to-day tech issues.

Key takeaway

Before you request a quote, count your users, list every software tool your team touches daily, and write down the last three tech problems that slowed you down. That 10-minute exercise will save you hours of back-and-forth and help any provider give you an accurate number — not a ballpark.

Hidden Costs Most Small Businesses Don't See Coming

The monthly fee is only part of the outsource IT for small business cost equation. Here's what tends to surprise business owners after they've signed a contract.

Onboarding and Setup Fees

Legitimate MSPs will spend real time documenting your systems, installing monitoring agents, and configuring security tools before they can support you properly. That work has a cost. Expect a one-time onboarding fee between $500 and $3,000 depending on complexity. Be skeptical of providers who waive this entirely — it usually means they're skipping the work, not absorbing the cost.

Out-of-Scope Work

Every managed services contract has a scope. Server migrations, new software integrations, SharePoint builds, AI automation setup — these are usually not included in a standard per-user fee. If your business is growing or changing, clarify upfront what triggers an additional invoice. At Sola AI Consulting, we build a clear scope into every engagement so clients aren't surprised when they ask for something new.

Software and Licensing Passthrough

Some providers mark up software licensing. Others pass it through at cost. Microsoft 365, security tools, backup software — these add up. A 10-person team might spend $150 to $400 per month on software alone, separate from any support fees. Ask every provider to itemize this clearly.

The Cost of Doing Nothing

This one doesn't show up on an invoice, but it's real. A staff member spending 45 minutes troubleshooting a printer issue, a nurse unable to access patient records for two hours, a billing coordinator manually re-entering data because two systems don't talk to each other — these are IT costs. They just look like labor costs on your books.

How to Evaluate Whether the Cost Is Worth It

The honest answer is that outsourcing IT is almost always worth it for small businesses with more than five employees — but only if you're buying the right service. Here's a simple way to evaluate any proposal.

  • Ask for references from businesses your size. A provider who mostly serves 200-person companies will price and scope differently than one built for teams of 10 to 50.
  • Request a sample scope of work before signing. Vague contracts create expensive disputes later.
  • Clarify response time guarantees. "We'll get back to you" is not an SLA. Know exactly what "priority" and "urgent" mean in their system.
  • Ask what happens in the first 30 days. A provider with a real onboarding process is worth more than one who just hands you a ticketing link.
  • Calculate your current downtime cost. If one hour of your team being unable to work costs $500 in lost productivity, and you experience that monthly, you're already spending $6,000 a year on bad IT — just invisibly.

Small businesses in industries like home health, legal services, and professional consulting often underestimate how much their operations depend on reliable, secure technology. For those clients especially, the outsource IT for small business cost conversation is really a conversation about risk — and what it costs when things go wrong versus what it costs to keep things running smoothly.

If you're trying to figure out the right model for your business — whether that's full managed services, a one-time infrastructure project, or a hybrid setup — a short conversation can usually get you to a clear answer faster than any pricing page will.
